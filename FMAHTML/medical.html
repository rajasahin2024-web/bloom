Medical English · with Future MedsAcademy

Doctors and nurses need a different English exam.

The Occupational English Test is the healthcare-specific alternative to IELTS, accepted by the GMC, NMC, AHPRA, the Medical Council of Ireland and dozens of other regulators. Every task is set in a clinical context — and that is exactly why it suits medics and why generic preparation fails.

Our advice: if your only goal is to practise, take OET — the vocabulary is already yours. If you also plan to study, take IELTS Academic, which covers both.
The four OET sub-tests
Listening · 45 min, all professions Patient consultations and a workplace presentation. Note-taking under clinical pressure.
Reading · 60 min, all professions Guidelines, package inserts, policy documents and journal extracts. Part A is fifteen minutes of pure speed.
Writing · 45 min, profession-specific One letter — usually a referral — written from real case notes. Selecting the relevant clinical detail is the whole skill.
Speaking · 20 min, profession-specific Two role-plays with an interlocutor playing your patient. Graded on clinical communication as well as language.
Twelve professions: medicine, nursing, dentistry, pharmacy, physiotherapy, occupational therapy, radiography, optometry, podiatry, speech pathology, veterinary science and dietetics.
How Bloom and FMA deliver it together

Language teachers and practising clinicians, in the same course.

OET fails candidates who have excellent clinical English and no idea what an examiner wants from a referral letter — and candidates whose English is fine but whose role-play manner is not. We put both specialists in the room.

  • Role-plays with real medicsFuture MedsAcademy clinicians play your patient, so the scenario behaves like a consultation rather than a language exercise.
  • Referral letters marked twiceOnce by a language examiner for the four criteria, once by a clinician for whether the letter would actually be useful to the receiving doctor.
  • Registration pathway includedGMC and NMC applications, PLAB and CBT preparation, and Italian and EU medical routes through Future MedsAcademy.
  • One account across both academiesYour Bloom profile and your FMA study record sit side by side — English on one side, clinical preparation on the other.
