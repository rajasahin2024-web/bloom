IELTS explained

Know the test before you sit it.

Most candidates lose marks to the format rather than to their English. Here is the whole exam laid out — what each section asks, how long you get, and what each band actually means to a university.

The nine-band scale

What your band actually means.

Bands are reported in whole and half scores. Your overall is the average of the four skills, rounded to the nearest half — which is why a single weak skill drags everything down.

9
Expert userFull operational command. Appropriate, accurate and fluent with complete understanding.
Rarely required. Some scholarships.
8
Very good userFully operational with only occasional unsystematic inaccuracies. Handles complex argument well.
Oxford, Cambridge, competitive law and medicine.
7
Good userOperational command with occasional inaccuracy. Handles complex language and detailed reasoning.
Most Russell Group postgraduate. Nursing and medical registration. Australia and Canada PR.
6.5
Competent, upperEffective command despite some inaccuracy. The single most requested score.
Standard postgraduate entry across the UK, Australia and Canada.
6
Competent userGenerally effective command; can use and understand fairly complex language in familiar situations.
Undergraduate entry, foundation and pathway programmes.
5.5
Modest, upperPartial command. Copes with overall meaning but makes many mistakes.
Foundation year, some vocational courses, pre-sessional English.
5
Modest userPartial command, handles basic communication in own field.
Pre-sessional English. Below most academic requirements.

Rounding: an average of 6.25 rounds up to 6.5; 6.1 rounds down to 6.0. Validity: results are normally accepted for two years. Retakes: unlimited, and single-skill retakes are now available at many centres.

Academic or General Training?

Choosing the wrong one is expensive.

Listening and Speaking are identical. Reading and Writing are not — and a university will not accept a General Training result, however high the band.

  • Take Academic for studyUniversity at any level, and professional registration in medicine, nursing, law, engineering and accountancy.
  • Take General Training for migrationPermanent residency and work visas for Australia, Canada, New Zealand and the UK, plus secondary school and vocational training.
  • If you are not certain, take AcademicIt is accepted for almost everything General is, but not the reverse. We check your target institution's requirement before you book.

How we do it

Five stages. Nothing is guessed.

Most preparation fails for one reason: students practise without ever checking what they got wrong. Bloom is built to make that impossible.

Placement test

Twenty-five minutes across grammar, vocabulary, reading, listening, writing and speaking. It returns a CEFR level, a band per skill, and your strongest and weakest areas — from what you actually did, not from what you told us.

Your English profile

An honest picture: where you sit on the CEFR scale, what your estimated band is on every exam scale, and which first-language errors we will be watching for as a speaker of your language.

A plan built from it

A day-by-day schedule to your test date, weighted towards your weakest skill, with a mock every Saturday and a checkpoint every fortnight. Tick each block off as you do it.

Practice with real feedback

Write an essay, get a band against all four descriptors and every error explained. Record a cue card, get fluency, vocabulary, grammar and pronunciation scored separately. Your recurring mistakes are collected into an error bank.

Measured, then adjusted

Every scored activity moves your estimate. When a mock says your reading is the problem, next week's plan changes. You always know what to revise today and what you have already mastered.
